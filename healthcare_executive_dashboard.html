Executive OverviewMay 2019 – May 2024 · 61 months

Executive Summary

Demand is evenly spread across conditions

Arthritis (9,218) and Diabetes (9,216) lead, but all six conditions sit within 1.3% of each other.

Implication: No single condition dominates volume; capacity planning should cover all six service lines rather than one.

Insurance mix is balanced

Cigna covers the most patients (11,139; 20.3%), only 317 above Aetna (10,822), the lowest.

Implication: Payer exposure is diversified; no provider is a dependency risk on volume alone.

Average billing is flat across ages

Patients aged 60+ average 25,563.59 versus 25,594.54 for all patients, a difference of about 0.1%.

Implication: Age alone does not distinguish cost on average; look at case mix and stay length instead.

One in three admissions is an emergency

18,102 emergency admissions were recorded (32.9% of 54,966); July is the busiest month (1,573) and February the quietest (1,452).

Implication: Emergency staffing needs are steady all year, with mild peaks to plan for.

Abnormal emergency results need triage

6,038 emergency cases (33.4% of emergency admissions) carry abnormal test results.

Implication: Prioritise review of this group first.

Management Priorities & Recommended Actions

What is happening?

  • 54,966 admissions across 5 years with balanced volume by condition and insurer.
  • Billing averages about 25,600 per admission and shows no clear age or time pattern.
  • Emergency admissions run about 1,500 a month.

Why does it matter?

  • Even demand means resources must be planned across all conditions.
  • A steady emergency flow with a July high affects staffing.
  • Abnormal emergency results and high-cost admissions concentrate clinical and financial risk.

What should management consider?

  • Set emergency staffing baselines near 1,500 per month with a summer buffer.
  • Triage the 6,038 abnormal emergency cases first.
  • Review the 5,497 high-cost admissions for drivers such as condition and stay length.
  • Investigate cost drivers beyond age.
